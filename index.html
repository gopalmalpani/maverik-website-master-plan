Typography Rules

Headings — Barlow Condensed

All H1–H4, nav labels, stat numbers, section labels. Weights: 800 (hero, H2), 700 (H3, cards), 600 (nav, tags). Letter-spacing: tight on display (-0.01em), slightly open on labels (+0.04em to +0.12em). Use text-transform: uppercase on micro-labels only.

Body — DM Sans

All body copy, descriptions, form fields. Weights: 400 (body), 500 (emphasis, table), 600 (subheadings). Font-size: 15px base, 13px secondary, 11px micro-labels. Line-height: 1.6–1.75 for paragraph text. Both fonts: Google Fonts, preloaded.

Spacing & Layout Rules

Max content width: 1100px. Side padding: 24px (mobile: 16px). Section padding: 60px top, 44px bottom. Card border-radius: 12px. Button border-radius: 8px. Tag/badge border-radius: 20px. Gap between cards: 16px. Gap between sections: enforced by section bottom border. CTA button height: 44px minimum for tap targets. All transitions: 0.2–0.25s ease.

Red dim overlay: rgba(253,37,33,0.10). Red border glow: rgba(253,37,33,0.25). Used for highlighted cards, action boxes, selected states.

The Brief

Apple announced Mac gaming support. The gaming community noticed. For most gaming hardware brands, a competitor announcement like that lands like a warning shot — a reason to go quiet and see what happens. HP India took the opposite call.

The brief to Maverik was direct: India's gaming community needs to hear from HP in the next 72 hours. Not a defensive response, not a counter-announcement — but an organic, creator-driven conversation that reaffirms why serious Indian gamers choose HP. The audience couldn't feel like they were being marketed at. It had to feel like their creators, who they already trusted, were just talking about what they use.

The constraint that made this hard: HP needed critical mass fast. A slow drip campaign wouldn't register next to Apple's announcement cycle. The only way to dominate the conversation was coordinated volume — enough creators, posting close enough together, that the HP narrative became the dominant gaming discourse for a 72-hour window.

The Approach

Maverik activated its full gaming creator network — the same creators who had already built audiences on gaming content, product reviews, and community commentary. The strategy had three moves:

  • Burst-window coordination: 200+ creators briefed on the same 72-hour content window. Not identical scripts — individual creators with individual voices — but coordinated timing so the aggregate volume created a conversation moment, not isolated posts.
  • Authentic over polished: The brief to creators was simple: show your HP setup, tell your audience why you use it for serious gaming, and keep it real. No brand-speak, no read-from-the-brief energy. Creators who tried to over-produce were coached back toward natural.
  • Tier architecture: Tier-1 gaming creators anchored the campaign with long-form YouTube content. Tier-2 and micro-creators amplified on Instagram and Shorts, creating the sense of a community-wide conversation rather than a paid media run.

TEC's (The Esports Club) creator relationships were the operational backbone — these weren't cold-briefed influencers. They were part of a network built over years of gaming community investment, which meant faster execution, higher content quality, and authenticity that paid media can't replicate.
